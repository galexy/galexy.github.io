I read a really good [article](https://harpers.org/archive/2026/03/childs-play-sam-kriss-ai-startup-roy-lee/) yesterday by Sam Kriss titled Child's Play. This article was so well written and it keep me engaged the whole time. It first, I was just really intrigued by his account of interviewing the CEO of the completely unhinged company Cluely. It's hard to not think that there is a bubble in the AI space when reading an account like this one. I also really wonder who is making the [investment decision at a16z](https://a16z.com/announcement/investing-in-cluely/)? The CEO seems really immature.

Sam highlights the irony of Roy Lee.

> [I found it strange that Roy couldn’t see the glaring contradiction in his own project. Here was someone who reacted very violently to anyone who tried to tell him what to do. At the same time, his grand contribution to the world was a piece of software that told people what to do.](https://harpers.org/archive/2026/03/childs-play-sam-kriss-ai-startup-roy-lee/#:~:text=say?”-,I,people%20what%20to%C2%A0do.,-There’s)

But, the really great part of this articles comes from Sam exploring what people with high agency (or highly agentic) are like. He interviews the rationalist Scott Alexander. And, describes the exploits of [Eric Zhu](https://ericzhu.com) (who supposedly zoomed with a Senator from a high school bathroom).
